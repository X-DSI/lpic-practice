## Context
LPIC-1 needs about 1,800 questions: 600 holdout plus about 300 practice per exam (mock-exam-composition
ADR). Each is generated by Claude from the LPI Learning Materials and must be checked twice and then
reviewed by the maintainer before it can appear in a mock. The workflow has to keep that review load
manageable, catch factual errors (CI cannot), and keep the record of who checked what.

## Options
- **One objective per PR** — 15 questions per weight point; small enough to review in one sitting,
  maps cleanly to a lesson and to `content/<exam>/questions/<objective>.yaml`.
- **One topic per PR** — fewer PRs, but 100–200 questions per review is too many to read properly.
- **One exam per PR** — unreviewable.

## Decision
1. **Batch.** One content PR per objective, on a `content/<exam>-<objective>` branch, adding or
   extending `content/<exam>/questions/<objective>.yaml`.
2. **Counts per objective.** `10 × weight` holdout and `5 × weight` practice questions.
3. **Holdout difficulty per objective** follows the bank-wide split from the difficulty rubric
   (about 16 / 22 / 24 / 21 / 16 % for levels 1–5), rounded to whole questions; for weight 1 that is
   2 per level. The mock assembler confirms the whole bank can fill the 10 mocks. Practice
   difficulty is unconstrained.
4. **Type mix**, across each exam: roughly 60–70 % `single_choice`, 15–25 % `multi_choice`,
   15–25 % `fill_in`. Individual objectives may vary.
5. **Pass 1 — against the source.** Every question is re-read against its lesson: the fact is right,
   the keyed answer is the only correct option, the wording is original (no Guided or Explorational
   Exercise restated), and the difficulty matches the rubric.
6. **Pass 2 — against reality.** Every command, option, path and behaviour a question relies on is
   run on a real Linux system, `tools/validate.py` passes, and each question is answered cold from
   its stem alone to catch ambiguity and giveaway distractors. Where the lesson and current
   behaviour disagree, the question follows current behaviour or avoids the point, and the PR says so.
7. **Recording.** Each pass adds a `review.history` entry (`stage`, `by`, `date`, `result`, `notes`).
   After both passes the status is `pass2`.
8. **Human sign-off.** The PR carries the `needs-human-review` label. The maintainer reviews the
   questions in the PR; on approval, a final commit adds a `human` history entry and sets
   `status: approved` before merge.

## Consequences
- About 120 content PRs for LPIC-1 (weights total 120); the PR list doubles as a progress tracker.
- Both passes are currently done by the same Claude session that wrote the questions. A later
  improvement is to run pass 2 in a fresh agent with no context from writing them.
- `verify` blocks are recorded on questions whose check runs in a stock `debian:stable-slim`
  container. Scenarios that need extra setup (compilers, root changes) are checked by hand in pass 2
  and described in the history notes, until a container runner exists.
- The `needs-human-review` label is only advisory until the CI check from PROTOCOL §8 exists.
