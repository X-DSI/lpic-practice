## Context
LPIC-1 needs about 1,800 questions: 600 holdout plus about 300 practice per exam (mock-exam-composition
ADR). Each is generated by Claude from the LPI Learning Materials. CI checks structure only, so
factual errors must be caught by review.

Two forces pull against a full human review of every question before merge:

- **Workload.** At one objective per PR, LPIC-1 is about 120 PRs of reading.
- **Holdout integrity.** Holdout questions exist so mock scores measure what the candidate knows.
  The maintainer is also the candidate: reading the holdout pool before taking the mocks would
  leak every mock question.

The trial batch (101 objective 102.3, PR #6) showed the writer's own two checks catch real errors —
5 of 15 questions needed a fix, including one with two correct answers and an answer key that was
always option `a` — but both checks were done by the writer, so anything it gets wrong twice slips
through.

## Options
- **One objective per PR, full human review** — most thorough, but too much reading, and it leaks the
  holdout pool.
- **The whole bank in one PR** — no diff review is possible at ~25,000 lines, one bad question
  blocks all of them, and generation spans many sessions anyway.
- **Topic-sized PRs, writer check + independent agent check, human sign-off in use** — about 10 PRs
  for LPIC-1; independence comes from a second agent; the human reviews questions when they would
  read them anyway.

## Decision
1. **Batch.** One content PR per exam topic (101: topics 101–104; 102: topics 105–110), on a
   `content/<exam>-topic-<topic>` branch, writing one `content/<exam>/questions/<objective>.yaml`
   per objective. A topic may be split across PRs if a session cannot finish it.
2. **Counts per objective.** `10 × weight` holdout and `5 × weight` practice questions.
3. **Holdout difficulty per objective** follows the bank-wide split from the difficulty rubric
   (about 16 / 22 / 24 / 21 / 16 % for levels 1–5), rounded to whole questions; for weight 1 that is
   2 per level. The mock assembler confirms the whole bank can fill the 10 mocks. Practice
   difficulty is unconstrained.
4. **Type mix**, across each exam: roughly 60–70 % `single_choice`, 15–25 % `multi_choice`,
   15–25 % `fill_in`. Correct answers are spread evenly across option positions.
5. **Pass 1 — writer, against the source.** The writing session re-reads every question against its
   lesson: the fact is right, the keyed answer is the only correct option, the wording is original
   (no Guided or Explorational Exercise restated), and the difficulty matches the rubric.
6. **Pass 2 — independent agent, against reality.** A separate agent that took no part in writing
   receives the questions **without** answers, answers each one cold, then compares with the key and
   tests every command, option, path and behaviour on a real Linux system. Its findings are fixed by
   the writer before the PR. Where the lesson and current behaviour disagree, the question follows
   current behaviour or avoids the point, and the PR says so.
7. **Recording.** Each pass adds a `review.history` entry (`stage`, `by`, `date`, `result`,
   `notes`); pass 2 entries name the independent reviewer in `by`. After both passes the status is
   `pass2`.
8. **Human sign-off happens in use, not in the PR.**
   - **Practice questions** are reviewed by the maintainer while studying: the CLI offers a way to
     flag a question, and flags become a fix list for the planner (PROTOCOL §10).
   - **Holdout questions** are reviewed on the results screen after the mock that contains them is
     taken. That review is the `human` history entry and moves the question to `approved`.
   - The maintainer does not need to read a content PR's questions before merge. Reviewing the PR
     summary (counts, what the checks found, lesson-vs-reality notes) is enough.
9. **Mocks may use `pass2` questions.** `tools/validate.py --strict` requires mock questions to be at
   least `pass2`, not `approved`, because approval of holdout questions only happens after the mock.

## Consequences
- About 10 content PRs for LPIC-1, each reporting what both checks found.
- The independent agent costs an extra review run per batch; it is the only check that is not the
  writer grading its own work.
- Errors that survive both checks reach the maintainer while studying. Flagging (practice) and
  post-mock review (holdout) are the backstop, and a flagged wrong answer is fixed in a small
  follow-up PR.
- Follow-up implementation work: change `--strict` (point 9), add question flagging to the CLI, and
  record post-mock review on the results screen.
